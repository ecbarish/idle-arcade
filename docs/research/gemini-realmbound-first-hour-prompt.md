# Gemini prompt: Realmbound's first hour

Requested by Evan; prepared by Codex, 2026-10-07. Copy the prompt below into Gemini Deep Research. Save the complete report, including sources, as `docs/research/realmbound-first-hour-research.md`. This sharpens the earlier classic-MMO prompt around observed opening risks rather than starting another feature wish list.

---

Research the first-play experience for Realmbound, an original single-player RPG inspired by classic MMO adventuring. We want the pleasure of entering a place, helping its people, learning a class, finding equipment and meeting useful companions. It must feel like a game world, not an idle dashboard. There is no real-money economy, multiplayer server or requirement to copy WoW's names, art or UI.

Current browser game: two factions, four races, five classes, levels 1–60, quests, talents, pets for Hunters, mounts, companion groups, walkable towns, an account-wide guild with jobs, five dungeons and a raid. Focus supports manual ability choices, while automatic combat/fallback also exists; earned addons handle things such as looting and quest assistance. Wildbond, our other game, is trying Godot, but Realmbound has not been committed to an engine migration. Separate experience recommendations from engine recommendations.

Observed opening, not speculation: the creator offers faction/race/class/journey/name. Creating a hero automatically accepts its first quest without playing the giver's request scene. Concord starts with Farmer Aldous's Wolves at the Fence; Wildclans starts with Elder Ugra's Lizards in the Water. Focus begins with its fallback already active. In four isolated accelerated 600-second unattended browser diagnostics, both factions' Warrior/Mage heroes reached the first quest's objective but turned in no quests: choosing a reward is still manual. These are randomized diagnostic observations, not human playthroughs or reliable pacing estimates. Later manually accepted quests have portrait dialogue. Town services already exist as places you can walk to.

Investigate:
1. How strong RPG/MMO openings establish who you are, where you are, an immediate human need and a clear next action. Compare a few concrete openings, not entire franchises. Explain what transfers from an MMO to single-player and what depends on real social players.
2. A welcoming first 5, 15 and 60 minutes for different classes. Teach resources, a usable ability, targets, loot, equipment, quest rewards, rest and travel in a sensible sequence. Address choice overload, unavailable abilities, reading time and an understandable first failure. Do not equate excitement with speeding up leveling.
3. Manual, assisted and automated play: how to communicate what the game is doing for the player, where agency matters and when earned convenience makes sense. Distinguish recommended policy from current behavior; do not assume changing a tutorial authorizes a core-loop redesign.
4. A populated single-player world: companion introductions, adventurers on the road, visible routines, group roles and first dungeon preparation. Study games such as Dragon's Dogma, Guild Wars' heroes/henchmen and FFXIV Duty Support using primary sources. Describe inexpensive rules-based techniques, their limits, and what to postpone. No promise of unrestricted AI chat or a whole simulated MMO.
5. Immersive yet readable interface and environmental guidance: where quest information belongs, how to show an actionable target, foreground people, readable light/contrast, optional help and clear feedback. Check keyboard/mouse, touch, reduced motion and large text. Cover ultrawide 3440x1440 without merely shrinking a menu column into a large window.
6. A practical newcomer study: recruit a small exploratory sample, observe without rescuing, test both faction starts and representative class/resource types. Define tasks and evidence: time to first deliberate action, understanding of automation, objective versus reward completion, requests for help, recovery after failure, and desire to continue. State that a small study discovers issues rather than proving a population-wide success rate.

Use current primary evidence: first-party manuals/documentation, developer talks/postmortems, official support explanations and clearly identified direct gameplay observations. Cite source links and dates/versions where available. Player reports may corroborate friction but must not establish implementation details or universal preferences. Flag unknown or unverified claims. If you cannot access our repository or play the game, state that; do not invent screenshots, player observations or source inspection.

Deliver: a concise recommendation; three alternative opening approaches with tradeoffs; a proposed event-based first-hour sequence with illustrative original dialogue labeled draft; a ranked table of ten improvements with risk, effort and acceptance tests; a keep/change/defer list; and a reproducible moderated playtest script. For each recommendation identify the observed problem, transferable evidence, project adaptation and how we would falsify it. Separate interface improvements from gameplay/save changes requiring approval. Do not prescribe more features as a substitute for proving the opening works.
