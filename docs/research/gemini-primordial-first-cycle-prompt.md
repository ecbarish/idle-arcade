Research the first session of Primordial, an original evolution-themed idle/progression browser game in a small passion-project arcade. The working game includes cell-to-large-creature progression, mutation choices, niche fights and extinction resets. The owner currently finds it less compelling than Starfall or the adventure games but sees a useful niche. It is on the back burner: research can help decide whether to revisit it, but is NOT permission to remove it or start a major redesign. Disclose whether you actually inspected or played github.com/ecbarish/idle-arcade/games/primordial/; do not assume it is unbuilt.

Investigate:
1. A distinct first-session fantasy: what does an adaptation choice change in how the organism survives, rather than just increasing a number? Compare specific documented evolution games and approachable progression/idle games; distinguish novelty from a repeatable enjoyable decision.
2. One adaptation cycle: simple organism, readable environmental pressure, two viable mutations, a visible consequence, one recoverable setback and a reason to continue. Propose three small loops without a full ecosystem, many new currencies or a prestige web.
3. Scientific honesty: evolution is not a fixed ladder with a predetermined destination. Identify where our game uses a fantasy metaphor, what wording/art could avoid misleading players, and which biological claims need primary scientific sources. Do not require an accurate biological simulator to make a satisfying game.
4. Active decisions versus appropriate idle rhythm: the player chooses mutations, strategy and resets; routine resource accumulation can suit this genre. Do not impose full unattended hero progression from another game or ban all idle behavior. Keep important choices deliberate; conveniences can be earned, but accessibility never has to be unlocked.
5. Reset expectations and attachment: what remains after extinction, what is lost, how do players know before confirming, and why is a new cycle desirable? Do not equate an accumulating prestige number with a fresh experience.
6. Presentation and testing: readable organism/pressure/outcome, one meaningful animation or lighting improvement, plain feedback, phone taps, larger text, reduced motion, keyboard and ultrawide. Identify whether presentation or the underlying choice is the larger problem before prescribing an engine.

Use primary game-developer/manual sources and primary scientific references for biological facts. Supply direct claim-level links with dates/versions; label observations, inferences and proposals. No invented repository inspection, completion rates or guarantees of retention. Keep platforms open; this game currently works in the browser. The approximate $200 budget is for the WHOLE project, with time/AI tokens as the main investment. Prefer existing tools and free assets; no paid purchases are authorized.

Deliver a first 10-minute journey, three tiny adaptation-loop alternatives, a ranked five-item table with effort/acceptance tests, a neutral newcomer script and clear reasons to keep/revisit/defer each approach. End with up to three owner decisions needed before implementing a new core loop. It is acceptable to conclude that the best next step is a short playtest or to leave the game on the back burner.
