# Review: Gemini's village builder and business tycoon report

Claude, 2026-10-08. The report: [village-and-business-research.md](village-and-business-research.md). Checked against
Starfall Guild (docs/plans/starfall-guild.md, games/starfall-guild/) and Evan's rules (CLAUDE.md, VISION §8).

## The short version

The most useful report so far. It understood the rules (no ads, automation earned, everything in the world) and its
best ideas match Evan's own words almost exactly: **you do the job yourself first, and hiring someone to do it is the
reward**; managers are people with habits, not multipliers; and a reset should move you somewhere new, not just multiply
a number. The village half fits **Starfall Guild** naturally (its party already follows prepared orders, like Majesty's
indirect control). A few technical suggestions are too heavy for us and are set aside.

## Take

| Idea | Why it fits us |
|---|---|
| **Do it yourself first; hiring is the reward** (chop wood, then hire a lumberjack you watch carry logs; bake 100 loaves before a bakery manager) | Exactly VISION §8 (earned automation that removes repetition, never the fun) |
| **Indirect control** (Majesty): post bounties on a board, price your shop; adventurers choose for themselves | Starfall already has prepared orders and autonomous parties; the board makes it physical |
| **Information shown in the world**: empty shelves, villagers carrying logs, coin piles that grow; small numbers (five logs look like five logs) | The in-game-window rule; readable in full words |
| **Soft quests from people** ("could you build three flower beds near the inn?"), never fail states | Fits "games feel like games" and a cozy town |
| **A tax collector early**, so you don't walk to every house (avoids the walking-simulator trap) | Same warning as the walk-in arcade review |
| **The town reacts to the outside** (fire elementals out there, a warm dry season in town) | One world, not separate systems (Cult of the Lamb lesson) |
| **Managers with personalities** (a fast stocker who drops things) | Delegating to characters (VISION §8), with a little friction that keeps you involved |
| **Mechanically different businesses** (stocking, cooking, machine repair) and **customers who change with the block** | Variety without reskins |
| **A reset as a move**: sell the block, take your favourite manager, start in a new, harder neighbourhood | A reason to restart that's a story, not a menu button |
| **Earned vehicles** for getting between businesses (bike, cart) | Evan asked for cars and conveniences |
| **Offline time**: a capped delivery wagon you unpack on return, never more than playing | Same as Realmbound's rested time; active play stays worth more |

## Set aside

- **Townscaper-style irregular grids and Wave Function Collapse:** beautiful but a large technical project; a simple
  grid with hand-drawn variety gets most of the charm. Revisit only if the town feels flat.
- **The idle-game formulas:** useful background (keep growth gentle, avoid exponential walls); the exact prestige
  equations weren't checked and aren't needed for a game without a multiplier treadmill.
- **"Make change" with coin rolls from the bank:** a fun detail for one business at most, not a system.

## Owner questions, with defaults

1. **Starfall becomes the village builder?** (Claude's recommendation: yes.) Still Evan's call.
2. **Adventurers who fall:** default **recovery** (they come back hurt and need care at your infirmary), with rare
   permanent loss only in a chosen hard mode. Attachment to people matters more than churn.
3. **Business failure:** default **cozy** (rent pauses until you recover, no losing a business), with a hard mode later.
4. **Moving between businesses:** walking at first, then an earned bike, then a cart: yes.
5. **Vertical integration** (carry tomatoes from your farm stand to your diner): yes, a natural delight.
