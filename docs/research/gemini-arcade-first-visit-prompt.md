Research the first visit to an original small arcade containing Wildbond (creature adventure), Realmbound (one-hero MMO-inspired adventure), Starfall Guild (prepare an adventuring party), Primordial (evolution/idle), Otherworld (a first-life story RPG) and Diamond Career (a first-payday baseball prototype in PR #45). These games have distinct fantasies; do not treat them as identical idle dashboards. The hub already has game cabinets, a living-world view, a walkable road, a hall, progress cards and feedback/votes. Research improvements to the existing launcher rather than prescribing its construction from scratch.

Wildbond's Godot trial/port is advancing while the browser remains a reference. Diamond Career and Otherworld explicitly start in the browser. The owner prefers the path of least resistance, keeps engines open and has an approximate $200 TOTAL project cash ceiling; most investment is time and AI tokens. We are not committed to wrapping every game, publishing on Steam or buying a launcher. No paid purchases or dependencies are authorized.

Investigate:
1. Discoverability: what does a new visitor think each game asks them to do, which title fits their mood, and where do they click/tap? Use specific console/indie launcher, game-box/manual and storefront examples. Distinguish good navigation from decoration that conceals a game. No unsupported claims that a layout universally increases conversion.
2. A five-minute arrival: choose, launch, see a clear first goal, act once, get a meaningful result, return and recognize saved progress. Separate prototype versus released-content expectations. Loading/errors/offline fallback must be understandable, without implying a browser and native save sync that does not exist.
3. Three existing presentation modes: living world, walkable road and hall/cabinets. Compare keyboard focus, touch targets, screen-reader links, readability, reduced motion and the 3440x1440 ultrawide. Identify whether the road adds pleasure or friction before proposing more movement or another interface mode.
4. Browser/native coexistence: clear Play versus Download/Run expectations, trust and launch friction, fullscreen, optional sound, safe return, save ownership and explicit import limits. Shared story links should be small delights, not required accounts or cross-game chores. Do not casually recommend a migration or installer as a styling fix.
5. Reusable playtesting: neutral newcomer observation, last understood step, recording interventions, failure recovery and checking a changed design with a fresh tester. Passing tests, self-playing demos and polished screenshots do not establish that a person can find a gate, understand a quest reward or choose a viable party. Separate chosen difficulty from confusing controls.
6. Turn findings into small tickets: bug versus design suggestion, severity, evidence, device/input context, reproduction steps, acceptance tests and ownership so parallel assistants avoid the same files. Feedback should be optional, with no saved-game contents or personal identifiers collected by default. An observation sheet is enough; analytics services are not required.

Lead with three first-visit risks and cheap tests. Use direct primary-source links, dates/versions and claim-level support. Disclose when our site/repository was not inspected and distinguish source facts, direct observations, inferences and suggestions. Avoid universal success percentages drawn from anecdotes.

Deliver a five-minute newcomer route, tradeoffs for the three existing modes, a ranked five-change table with acceptance tests, a reusable 20-minute cross-game playtest sheet and a severity rubric. Keep recommendations attainable with existing shared systems and free tools. End with up to three real owner decisions, not a shopping list or a demand to rebuild every game in one engine.
