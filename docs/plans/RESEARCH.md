# Comparable-game research

Researched 2026-10-06 for Evan's Idle Arcade. These are desk reviews of published criticism, developer descriptions and sampled player reviews, not claims that Codex personally played every game. Steam's most-helpful reviews are a selected sample, not a representative survey. Old reviews describe the version/platform reviewed; criticism may not apply to later patches. No sales or retention claim is inferred from these sources.

The tables separate reported strengths/problems from our proposed design response. Responses are hypotheses to test in our games. Keep names, stories, art and implementations original. References below link to the retrieved sources; recommendations do not require buying these games.

## Realmbound: connected progression without a maintenance burden

| Comparator | What worked in the reviewed evidence | What did not / limitation | Proposed response |
|---|---|---|---|
| IdleOn | Account-wide idle progression and a broad set of activities underpin its attraction; sampled long-time reviewers describe previously enjoying that growth. | Some long-time Steam reviewers describe accumulating chores, micromanagement and paid progression pressure. This is reported sentiment, not a finding about all players. [R1] | Let alts support one another, but give assignments sensible defaults and a useful return summary. Earn automation; avoid recurring compulsory chores. |
| Melvor Idle | One review praises skills feeding other skills and combat offering additional depth. [R2] | Another reviewer found early progression too open and difficult to navigate. [R3] | Begin with one production chain and a visible next goal. Do not unlock ten unexplained professions at once. |

Our strongest direction is an adventuring account with a small supporting economy. The roster should add choices: who explores, gathers and crafts, and what the player actively controls. It should not multiply the number of maintenance clicks by the number of heroes.

## Wildbond: attachment, team strategy and a ranch with purpose

| Comparator | What worked | What did not / limitation | Proposed response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monster Sanctuary | RPGFan praises team options, skill combinations, exploration and pixel presentation. | It also notes endgame grind and unwieldy party/quest management. [R4] | Teach a few understandable team combinations and make retraining inexpensive enough to experiment. Keep basic creatures useful. |
| Cassette Beasts | Its review praises customizable moves, fusion, characterful designs and music. | The reviewer found early movement slow, element interactions difficult to follow, and encounters disruptive during exploration. [R5] | Show why a command works; allow intentional exploration without incessant interruption. Test mobility and readable elemental feedback before adding many more species. |
| Pocket Stables | The developer describes horses with different attributes, training facilities and races; the review finds the game understandable. [R6, R7] | Pocket Gamer criticizes slow training/racing repetition and limited depth around replacing aging horses. Evan enjoyed its raising fantasy; the critic's judgment need not match his. [R7] | Keep raising and bloodlines, but add track-specific tactics, meaningful training tradeoffs and a useful retirement role rather than repeated stat gates. |

Battle remains Wildbond's main spine. Racing, ranch jobs and contests should make the creatures you already care about more valuable, not demand separate optimized collections for every activity.

## Primordial: adaptations that change survival

| Comparator | What worked | What did not / limitation | Proposed response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spore | GameSpot praises approachable creation tools and attachment to the creatures made with them. | The same review describes breadth over depth, and a loss of intimacy as play moves into civilization/space. [R8] | Make the organism visibly reflect choices. Finish one ecological chapter before promising several unrelated simulations. |
| Niche | Its developer defines genetics-driven species survival; Finger Guns finds tension in feeding a population while avoiding predators. [R9, R10] | That Switch review reports repetitive openings, weak reasons to leave safe islands and unclear navigation; some control complaints are port-specific. [R10] | Make changing habitat rewarding and understandable. Give the next environment a visible opportunity as well as a threat. |
| Thrive | Its project history records a staged evolution ambition and eventually finishing the microbe stage. | The history records difficult transitions into 3D and a long development journey. This is a scope/process case, not proof the current game is bad. [R11] | Treat multicellular and land stages as individually playable milestones. Upgrade only after the current stage demonstrates replayable choices. |

The opportunity is a readable evolutionary survival game where a different environment makes a different adaptation useful. A larger multiplier alone does not demonstrate adaptation.

## Starfall Guild: a guild whose members have careers

| Comparator | What worked | What did not / limitation | Proposed response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dungeon Village 2 | Sampled Steam reviews praise watching adventurers grow, improving the town and taking progress into another village. | Several sampled reviews, including positive ones, criticize excessive pausing popups and uneven pacing; PC control complaints should not be generalized to every version. [R12] | Make the town and roster visibly active. Batch routine news; reserve interruptions for an actual decision. |
| Wildermyth | The Xbox review praises character investment, writing and tactical campaigns. | It reports repeated events and disappointment when a familiar pair's relationship did not carry into a new campaign. Bugs mentioned are specific to that review's Xbox build. [R13] | Preserve a member's identity and a small career record across seasons. Track seen events and make repeat events react to history. |
| Darkest Dungeon | RPGFan describes party construction around positions, ailments and stress as engaging. | The review criticizes opaque numbers, punitive randomness and slow late progression. [R14] | Borrow roster tradeoffs and legible combinations. Use a lighter recovery system, explained setbacks and fewer rebuild chores. Do not import its horror tone automatically. |

Starfall should be the game about directing several adventurers and building a guild. Realmbound remains the game about inhabiting a hero in a larger adventuring account.

## Diamond Career: understandable advancement, short playable moments

| Comparator | What worked | What did not / limitation | Proposed response |
|---|---|---|---|
| MLB The Show 25: Road to the Show | The official description puts an amateur origin and controllable player development before professional progression. [R15] | SB Nation's review describes frustration with unclear promotion feedback and manager messages inconsistent with the writer's performance. That is one career experience, not a proven universal promotion algorithm. [R16] | Show promotion requirements, opportunities and evaluations. Make narrative feedback agree with simulated performance. |
| Baseball Superstars 2012 | Gamezebo describes batter/pitcher careers, seasonal goals and training/rest/equipment around matches. | The review says care systems can become overwhelming, training is slow, and perceived pitch speeds can be confusing. This is a historical mobile design reference. [R17] | Start with a few readable decisions between games and clear pitch feedback. Keep salary useful without many upkeep meters. |
| Super Mega Baseball 4 | Reviews praise accessible presentation combined with traits, team chemistry and Franchise decisions. | Push Square describes a familiar fourth entry; richer menus do not alone reinvent the playing experience. Franchise is a team-management reference, not a comparable player-career mode. [R18] | Use original teams and distinctive player traits. Add club management only after the single-player career works. |

Our differentiator is living a baseball career through consequential short moments and transparent decisions, with simulation for the remainder. It is not a plan to reproduce an entire licensed baseball simulation.

## Otherworld: a life worth living again

| Comparator | What worked | What did not / limitation | Proposed response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loop Hero | Nintendo Life finds the compact loop of decisions, changing encounters and expedition management satisfying. | It criticizes increasingly costly facility progression and repetitive resource accumulation. [R19] | Let each life alter the problems and options. Use rebirth to change a build/story route, rather than repeat the same chores for a permanent multiplier. |
| Your Chronicle | Its developer describes a text-based idle RPG with story actions, automated battles, summons and unlockable automation. [R20] | Sampled Steam reviews describe small resource capacities and production chains requiring repeated manual switching. These are player experiences, not measurements of the latest balance. [R21] | Actions should produce story choices or real build changes. Avoid a chain that needs a click every few minutes while calling itself idle. |
| Wildermyth | Its review demonstrates the value of characters accumulating meaningful experiences. | Repeating events and broken relationship continuity weakened that attachment for the reviewer. [R13] | Use knowledge from former lives in later dialogue and preserve intentional soul memories. Explain what a rebirth keeps and what it loses. |

One protagonist, evolving skills and narrative consequences should distinguish Otherworld from both the guild manager and the MMO-style roster game.

## Expanded sports brief: career income, lifestyle and management

Evan clarified the multi-sport, player-to-management and meaningful-contract direction during research. This expands the comparison set beyond the initial baseball plan.

| Comparator | What worked | What did not / limitation | Proposed response |
|---|---|---|---|
| New Star Soccer | Pocket Gamer praises the mixture of short player-controlled moments, career growth and wages spent on lifestyle items including homes. [R23] | A later Android-era review complains that energy costs, expiring hired services and degrading lifestyle items drained earnings. This is a dated version-specific account. [R24] | Contracts should fund permanent visible purchases; avoid an upkeep treadmill that consumes every pay rise. |
| New Star Manager | Nintendo Life praises tactile attacking moments mixed with team/facility decisions. [R25] | Nintendo Insider reports an easy recurring scoring tactic and frustration with not controlling defense. [R26] | Separate match and management layers, support counterplay, and do not label attacking highlights as full manual matches. |
| NBA 2K25 MyCareer/The City | GameSpot describes a compelling player-career/social-world combination. | The critic argues its paid attribute economy makes a low-rated player's experience frustrating. [R27] | Make wealth express a career through assets and opportunities; earn sporting competence through play rather than copying a paid-stat shortcut. |
